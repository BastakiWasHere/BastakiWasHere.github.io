## Why I chose Iceland

I chose Iceland because it has many distinctive features for a small country. I found the contrast between its name, “Iceland,” and its volcanoes especially interesting. Glaciers, hot springs, and waterfalls also caught my attention. Before beginning the project, I had noticed the importance of the coast to where people live. These interests led me to a question: how does Iceland's landscape relate to where people live and travel?

I selected waterfalls, glaciers, hot springs, volcanoes, and casinos for this map. The first four categories reflect my interest in Iceland's landscape. The fifth adds a human place to the comparison. Together, the five layers provide a starting point for my question.

## Reading the map

The controls allow each category to be shown or hidden. Clicking a point opens its name, GeoNames code, coordinates, recorded elevation, timezone, modification date, and original record. Colors distinguish the categories, while larger markers make the volcano and casino layers easier to find. The reset button returns to the full Iceland view.

<div class="note">Scope note: these are gazetteer records, not a complete inventory. The five layers do not include population, travel routes, visitor counts, or volcano activity status. They cannot by themselves establish settlement patterns, travel behavior, or whether a volcano is active. Háspenna is classified as a casino in GeoNames; its record was last modified in 2018 and current operation is unverified.</div>

## What maps show—and leave out

*Do Maps Lie?* made me think about how a map can highlight some things while leaving other things out. That question matters to this project because I chose which features would be visible. This map brings five categories to the foreground, while other features remain outside its scope. My next step is to develop this observation using specific examples from my own exploration and the course material.

## Methods and sources

The technical workflow imports `IS.txt` using the 19-column GeoNames schema, filters the selected feature codes in R, builds Leaflet layers, and exports a self-contained HTML widget. Basemap images still require an internet connection. The additional feature layers and interface customizations extend the supplied notebook.
